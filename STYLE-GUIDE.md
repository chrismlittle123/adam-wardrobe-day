# Style Guide: Adam

## The thesis
This wardrobe is built on a philosophy of quiet confidence. It channels the relaxed elegance of 1990s Milan and the cinematic warmth of mid-century Rome, using a tight palette of rich, earthy colours that complement your skin tone. The focus is on exceptional fabric and a silhouette that is sharp in the shoulder and relaxed through the body and leg. Every piece is chosen for comfort and texture, creating outfits that feel easy, sharp, and warm. Because aesthetics (9/20) are your priority over cost (2/20), this is about buying fewer, better things that you will wear for years.

## What it should say
**At work:** Competent and senior. This is achieved not with stiff suits, but with the authority of good cloth. Unstructured blazers, fine-gauge knits, well-cut wool trousers and clean leather shoes project authority without trying. The quality of the material does the talking for you.

**Weekends:** That you have a rich life beyond a screen. This is about moving from the default uniform of jeans and a tee to something more considered but equally comfortable. Suede jackets, textured knitwear, and relaxed trousers in cotton or linen show intention and taste. It is the difference between dressing for the day you have and the day you want.

## What it must never say
*   Trying too hard.
*   A costume.
*   A finance man on his day off.
*   Generic, default, or lazy.
*   Loud, flashy, or logo-driven.
*   Anything that relies on a black shirt.

## Silhouette and fit
Your lean, athletic frame is the ideal canvas for a relaxed, tapered silhouette.

*   **Shoulders:** The anchor point. Jackets, coats, and shirts must fit cleanly on the shoulder. Unstructured or soft shoulders are best; they accommodate your frame without pulling across the back.
*   **Sleeves:** Must end at the wrist bone. Most off-the-peg shirt sleeves will be too long; find a brand that fits or factor in £15 for an alteration. It is the sharpest detail.
*   **Trousers:** This is the key change. Move to a mid or high rise. This elongates the leg and is more comfortable. The cut should be straight or have a gentle taper, with room in the thigh. This solves your waist/thigh fit problem by design. Aim for no break or a slight "shivering" break over the shoe.
*   **Volume:** Volume goes in the leg of the trouser and the drape of a knit or jacket. Nothing should be tight or clinging, especially around your middle, as you prefer. The overall shape is a soft 'V', broad at the shoulder and easy through the body.

## Fabric and texture
Texture provides the interest that patterns do not. Because you will not iron, fabric choice is paramount.

*   **Embrace:**
    *   **Wool:** Your best friend. Merino for knits, flannel and high-twist wool for trousers. Naturally resists wrinkles and odour.
    *   **Suede:** For outerwear and shoes. Adds instant warmth and texture.
    *   **Heavy Cotton:** Twill, canvas, and moleskin for trousers and jackets. They hold their shape and wear in, not out.
    *   **Linen & Linen Blends:** For summer shirts and trousers. The wrinkles are part of the charm; do not fight them.
    *   **Cashmere Blends:** For knitwear and overcoats. Unbeatable for softness and warmth without bulk.
*   **Avoid:**
    *   Shiny synthetics. Polyester, nylon, and elastane-heavy fabrics look and feel cheap. Check the label.
    *   'Performance' fabrics outside the gym.
    *   Thin, flimsy cotton that creases instantly and looks poor.

## Retire
*   **Three black going-out shirts:** Black is not in your palette. It is harsh against your skin tone and rarely looks expensive unless it is cashmere or wool. These are dated.
*   **Boxy navy puffer:** It makes you "look square", which is the opposite of the silhouette we are building. It lacks the elegance your aesthetic requires.
*   **Grey marl joggers:** For public wear. They read as lazy and are the uniform of a man who has given up. Keep them for the gym or the sofa, never for the street.

## Shopping list
Your score of 2/20 for cost means investing in quality. These prices reflect that. Buy the best you can afford; it will look, feel, and last better.

1.  **Grey Flannel Trousers**

3.  **Brown Loafers**

    *   **Price:** £200-£400

4.  **Wool Overcoat**
    *   **What:** Single-breasted, notch lapel, in camel, navy, or charcoal. Wool/cashmere blend. Must fit over your blazer.
    *   **Price:** £500-£900
    *   **Unlocks:** Your 'proper' adult coat. Allows you to look sharp and stay warm from autumn to spring without defaulting to a puffer.

## Rules of thumb
1.  Fit the shoulder first. The rest can be altered.
2.  If it needs ironing, you will not wear it. Be honest in the shop.
3.  Check the fabric label. If it says polyester, put it back.
4.  Is the rise high and the thigh relaxed? If not, they are the wrong trousers.
5.  When in doubt, wear the loafers.
6.  Does this feel more like Mastroianni or a man in a costume?

## Open questions
*   Your hard "no ironing" rule is a major constraint, particularly for cotton shirts. Would you use a steamer? A quick steam is less effort and opens up more options for high-quality cotton shirting that isn't chemically treated to be non-iron.
*   Your office dress code is undefined. Are jeans and trainers acceptable? Knowing the baseline would help refine the 'Work' uniform and determine if we need more formal trousers or shoes.
*   Cost is a low priority (2/20), but "low" is not a number. A rough annual budget for clothing would allow for more specific brand recommendations and help prioritise spending. Is £2,000 a year realistic? £5,000?
