# Style Guide: Adam

## The thesis
This wardrobe is built on a foundation of 70s American cool, updated for a modern European city. It has two distinct modes: a relaxed, textured daily uniform inspired by skater and surfer subcultures, and a sharper, more sensual look for evenings and dates that channels your style icons. It is varied by design, allowing you to be appropriate for the context without ever being generic. Every piece is chosen to flatter your athletic frame and warm colouring. This is not streetwear.

## What it should say
**On a date or dancing:** "Sexy, memorable." This is the Kravitz/Butler mode. Think leather, shirts with a relaxed drape and a few buttons undone, smart trousers and boots. The gold jewellery is central here. The fit is close but not tight, showing off your physique without shouting.

**Commuting or going to Tesco:** "Happy, considered." This is the relaxed skater/surfer vibe. It is built from high-quality, textured basics: heavyweight T-shirts, corduroy, denim, and canvas. The silhouette is relaxed but intentional, using slightly oversized layers on top to accentuate your V-taper.

**At a festival:** "Practical, but still you." This is where your active life and your style meet. It uses technical fabrics in a considered way, paired with robust pieces like your sleeveless denim jacket and sturdy boots. It’s comfortable for moving but looks put-together.

## What it must never say
*   I am wearing my gym clothes to go out.
*   This was the cheapest and most convenient option.
*   I am a student in a shapeless hoodie.
*   I am trying to look like a sponsored mountain climber.
*   This could belong to anyone.

## Silhouette and fit
Your physique is your biggest asset. The goal is to accentuate your V-taper.

*   **Trousers:** Mid-rise, regular fit. The leg should be straight or have a gentle taper. They should sit on your shoe with no break or a very slight one, which keeps the line clean and works with your long limbs. This is why your Dickies work.
*   **Tops:** T-shirts and sweatshirts should be a 'classic' or 'regular' fit, not muscle-fit. The cloth should skim your torso, not cling to it. Sleeve length is critical for your long arms; always check it hits at or just beyond the wrist bone.
*   **Outerwear:** Jackets must have structure in the shoulder. They should be slightly oversized in the body but not sloppy. The length must be at the hip, as you specified. This combination—structured shoulder, looser body, cropped length—is the primary tool for accentuating your V-taper.

## Fabric and texture
Texture makes simple outfits memorable. You love corduroy and leather, and hate polyester. We will build on that.

*   **Embrace:** Heavyweight cotton jersey for T-shirts, corduroy, leather, suede, heavyweight denim, wool (especially flannel and structured twills), linen, and Tencel or silk blends for shirts. These fabrics have character and feel good against the skin.
*   **Upkeep:** You are fine with ironing and hand-washing, so natural fibres are no issue.
*   **Avoid:** Thin, flimsy cottons. All forms of polyester and cheap synthetics that look shiny. Generic fleece. Anything that feels like a "performance" fabric unless it is for an explicitly practical purpose (e.g., your hiking trousers for a festival).

## Retire
Remove these from your wardrobe. They are actively working against your goals.

*   **"Tea towel looking Primark overshirt"**: It looks and feels cheap, undermining the "memorable" and "sexy" goals. It lacks the quality and texture that defines the new wardrobe.
*   **"Navy parachute trousers"**: These fall into the generic streetwear category you want to avoid for your casual look. The fabric and cut are at odds with the textured, natural-fibre direction we are taking.

## Shopping list
Prioritised to fill the most significant gaps first. Your priority is Cost (7), then Aesthetics (6), so we are looking for high-street-plus quality and value, not designer labels.

1.  **Charcoal Wool Trousers**: Look for a regular fit with a straight leg. Check Cos, Arket, or Reiss (on sale). **Budget: £80-150.** This is your most urgent gap for smart contexts and unlocks the "Date" uniform.
2.  **Heavyweight T-shirts (x3)**: In off-white, charcoal, and olive. Look for 200gsm+ cotton. Brands like Uniqlo U, Carhartt WIP, or ISTO. **Budget: £25-40 each.** These are the new foundation for 80% of your outfits.
3.  **Camp Collar Shirts (x2)**: In a silk-blend or Tencel. One plain (terracotta/petrol blue), one patterned. Check AllSaints (on sale), Percival, or Wax London. **Budget: £70-120 each.** These immediately deliver the 70s/Kravitz vibe.
4.  **Black Leather Boots**: A simple, almond-toe Derby boot. Avoid anything too pointy or chunky. Check Dr. Martens (the 'Made in England' line), Solovair, or Clarks. **Budget: £140-200.** These will elevate every pair of trousers you own.
5.  **Wool Overshirt**: A thick, textured wool-blend in charcoal. Check Universal Works, Folk, or & Other Stories (menswear). **Budget: £100-180.** This replaces the Primark shirt and becomes your go-to casual jacket.

## Rules of thumb
*   Warm colours first. Cream over white, charcoal over black.
*   If the fabric is boring, the outfit is boring. Prioritise texture.
*   Does this accentuate the V-taper? Check the shoulders and the length.
*   Is this sexy, memorable, or happy? If not, do not buy it.
*   Check the sleeve length before you check the price tag.
*   If it looks like it's sold at an airport shop, leave it there.
